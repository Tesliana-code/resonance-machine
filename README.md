Resonance Machine

A privacy-first research project in embodied AI.

Can a machine learn presence without watching a person?

Resonance Machine explores whether inexpensive, privacy-conscious sensors can produce a meaningful representation of presence, movement, rhythm, and the interaction between a person and physical space.

The project starts from a simple research question:

Can an artificial system learn the continuity of physical presence without relying on conventional visual surveillance?

Rather than asking who is this person?, the project asks how the physical environment changes because someone is present, and whether multiple weak signals can form a useful representation when synchronized over time.

Research direction

Prototype V1 is intentionally modest. The initial experiments aim to study whether multimodal sensing can reliably distinguish:

an empty room from an occupied room

stillness from movement

several body postures

repeated walking patterns

longer-term changes in environmental rhythm

The goal is not perfect recognition. The goal is to test whether synchronized multimodal sensing can produce stable representations that no single sensor provides on its own.

Privacy as a design constraint

Resonance Machine is designed around consent and data minimization from the beginning.

Core principles include:

voluntary participation and informed consent

local processing whenever possible

minimal storage of raw signals

encrypted research datasets

transparent deletion policies

no hidden deployment

no surveillance applications

A system capable of sensing presence should be designed with consent before capability.

Research roadmap

Build the first synchronized multimodal sensing prototype.

Collect a carefully controlled volunteer dataset.

Develop baseline models for presence, movement, posture, and repeated behavioral rhythms.

Explore stable high-dimensional representations of person-space interaction over time.

Publish findings, limitations, and future research directions.
